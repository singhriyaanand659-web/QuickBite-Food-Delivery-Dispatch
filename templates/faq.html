Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about QuickBite orders, dispatch algorithms, payment methods, cancellation and refunds.

📦 Orders

💳 Payments

QuickBite supports Cash on Delivery (COD), UPI (Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm, BHIM, QR code), and all major Credit and Debit Cards (Visa, MasterCard, RuPay). In this environment, all online payments use an authentic realistic demo authorization flow without charging real currency.

❌ Order Cancellation

Orders can be cancelled at any point during the "Placed", "Confirmed", "Preparing", or "Rider Assigned" stages directly from the My Orders or Tracking page. Once an order is marked "Picked Up" or "On The Way", online self-cancellation is closed to prevent food wastage.

QuickBite offers 100% free cancellation for orders cancelled within the allowed window. Any prepaid amount is automatically queued for immediate refund.

💰 Refunds

When you cancel a prepaid order (UPI or Card), our system immediately creates a Refund Record and progresses through 3 transparent states: Refund Initiated → Processing → Completed. You can track this in real-time on our dedicated Refund Tracker page.

For Cash on Delivery orders, no upfront money was deducted, so no monetary refund is necessary. The order is simply marked cancelled and rider dispatch is halted.

🚚 Delivery

QuickBite covers prime Mumbai graph nodes: Bandra, Dadar, Andheri, Kurla, BKC, Ghatkopar, Powai, Goregaon, and Thane. All road weights represent realistic travel times calculated by our graph algorithms.

📍 Live Tracking & Dijkstra Algorithm

Our backend models the city as a weighted LocationGraph. When an order is assigned, Dijkstra's algorithm finds the mathematical minimum-time path for Leg 1 (Rider → Restaurant) and Leg 2 (Restaurant → Customer), ensuring minimal ETA.

The 7 stages are: 1. Order Placed → 2. Confirmed → 3. Preparing → 4. Rider Assigned → 5. Picked Up → 6. On The Way → 7. Delivered. You can follow live updates along this timeline on the Tracking page.

🚴 Riders & Min-Heap Dispatch

Rather than an inefficient linear scan, our dispatch engine pushes all reachable idle riders into a binary Min-Heap keyed by Dijkstra travel time to the restaurant. Extracting the root node in O(log N) guarantees the optimal closest rider is assigned instantly.
